# Instructor Manual — Course Delivery, Safety and Assessment

**Course:** Computer Fundamentals and Artificial Intelligence Foundation Course  
**Academy:** Next Generation Academy  
**Location:** Seni Gumbat, Kohat, Pakistan  
**Duration:** 8 weeks | 40 lessons | 80 guided hours  
**Delivery:** Instructor-led classroom and computer lab  
**Language:** Simple English  
**Learning balance:** Approximately 20% explanation and 80% demonstration, guided practice and student practice

> **Document status:** This is the operational instructor manual for planning and delivering the approved course. The academy should review local safeguarding, privacy, health-and-safety, software-licensing and child-protection requirements before each delivery. This manual is not a substitute for manufacturer instructions, qualified technical support or applicable law.

## 1. Purpose and instructor responsibility

The instructor's role is to help beginners become safe, confident and increasingly independent digital learners. The aim is not to rush through software menus. Students should understand what they are doing, practise it, explain the result and know when to stop and ask for help.

The instructor is responsible for:
- Preparing the room, devices, accounts, lesson materials and safe practice environment.
- Teaching the approved 40-lesson curriculum in sequence unless the academy formally approves a change.
- Demonstrating each unfamiliar task before students practise it.
- Protecting students, their data, academy equipment and online accounts.
- Monitoring individual participation and practical progress.
- Assessing work fairly and keeping accurate records.
- Correcting misconceptions and unsafe habits promptly.
- Preparing alternatives when internet access, software, accounts or devices are unavailable.
- Communicating progress and concerns through academy-approved channels.

Never claim that students have mastered a skill simply because the instructor demonstrated it. Mastery requires observable student evidence.

## 2. Course structure and teaching standard

### 2.1 Approved programme

| Week | Main focus | Expected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Computer fundamentals and hardware | Component identification, safe inspection and labelled diagrams |
| 2 | Windows and computer operations | File/folder organisation, settings and peripheral use |
| 3 | Windows management, installation, internet and networking | Safe system observation, storage concepts, network map, browser/email practice |
| 4 | Microsoft Office and digital productivity | Word, Excel and PowerPoint outputs |
| 5 | AI foundations and professional prompt engineering | Tested prompt library and responsible AI decisions |
| 6 | AI for learning, research and office productivity | Source-grounded research and integrated office outputs |
| 7 | AI graphic design, video and digital content | Poster, thumbnail, content plan and short-video output/prototype |
| 8 | AI-assisted website development, GitHub and career readiness | Student website, repository, README and deployment evidence |

Each lesson is 120 minutes. Use the lesson file for the specific outcomes, terminology, teaching sequence, activities, homework and checklist.

### 2.2 Standard 120-minute lesson

| Time | Standard | Instructor action |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 min | Welcome and retrieval | Attendance, review previous learning, introduce today's goal |
| 10–20 min | Essential theory | Explain only the concepts needed for the practical task |
| 20–35 min | Instructor demonstration | Project the steps slowly; explain decisions and safety |
| 35–75 min | Guided practice | Students follow in small steps; instructor circulates |
| 75–100 min | Independent or paired practice | Each student completes the task and produces evidence |
| 100–110 min | Review and troubleshooting | Correct common errors; invite students to explain their work |
| 110–120 min | Assessment and close | Exit questions, checklist, homework and safe shutdown |

The timings are a teaching framework, not a reason to abandon a student who needs more support. If a task is complex, reduce optional extension work rather than skipping essential safe practice.

### 2.3 Practical-first teaching cycle

Use this cycle for each new skill:
1. **Explain:** state what the tool/task is for and why it matters.
2. **Show:** demonstrate on the instructor device, one step at a time.
3. **Think aloud:** explain how you decide what to click, check or avoid.
4. **Practise together:** students repeat the steps with support.
5. **Practise independently:** each student completes a similar task.
6. **Check:** ask the student to explain the result and demonstrate it again.
7. **Reflect:** identify what is secure and what needs more practice.

Do not rely only on “Does everyone understand?” Ask students to show or explain a step.

## 3. Before the course begins

### 3.1 Student readiness and orientation

Assume students may have very different levels of digital experience. Some may not yet know how to:
- Use a mouse, keyboard, touchpad or common keyboard shortcuts.
- Open and close a program or browser tab.
- Create, name and find a file or folder.
- Download a file safely.
- Understand an email address, password or verification code.
- Distinguish a browser from a search engine.
- Use a cloud account or GitHub.

Do not embarrass students for missing these skills. Use a short, low-pressure readiness activity and provide extra guided practice. The course begins at beginner level; prior programming knowledge is not required.

### 3.2 Student orientation

Explain:
- Course duration, timetable, attendance and participation expectations.
- How to use the repository and course materials.
- How to save work using the academy's agreed folder structure.
- How practical work and assessment are recorded.
- Safe use of equipment, accounts, internet and AI tools.
- How to request help and report a safety or privacy concern.
- That AI output can be wrong and must be checked.

Do not ask students to publish personal phone numbers, home addresses, private photos or sensitive details as part of a project.

### 3.3 Baseline skills record

During the first week, observe each student's ability to use the mouse/keyboard, open a program, follow a projected demonstration, create a folder, save a file and explain a simple instruction. Record support needs privately. This is a starting point, not a graded judgement.

## 4. Classroom management and inclusive teaching

### 4.1 Classroom expectations

Agree on a small set of visible rules:
- Arrive on time and prepare the workstation.
- Follow the instructor's safety directions.
- Use academy equipment and internet for approved learning tasks.
- Ask before installing software, changing settings or connecting devices.
- Respect other students' work, privacy and turn-taking.
- Keep food and drinks away from computers.
- Save work regularly and sign out of shared accounts.
- Leave the workstation clean and report faults immediately.

Use calm, consistent correction. Explain the risk and the correct action; do not shame or publicly label a student.

### 4.2 Seating and grouping

Use flexible groups rather than fixed ability labels:
- **Pairs:** useful for mouse/keyboard practice, peer explanation and checking work.
- **Groups of three:** assign rotating roles such as operator, step-reader and checker. Rotate roles so each student practises.
- **Individual practice:** required to confirm that every learner can perform the skill.
- **Instructor demonstration:** use a projected device so all students can see the same steps.

Do not allow one confident student to do all the work. Every student must take a turn operating the computer and explaining the outcome.

### 4.3 Differentiation

For students who need more support:
- Break instructions into one action per line.
- Provide printed screenshots or a step checklist.
- Repeat the demonstration at a slower pace.
- Use prepared practice files and partially completed examples.
- Pair them with a supportive peer while retaining individual turns.
- Check understanding privately and frequently.

For students who finish early:
- Ask them to explain the task in their own words.
- Give a realistic extension or troubleshooting scenario.
- Ask them to compare two methods and justify the safer choice.
- Let them support a peer only after their own work is complete.

Do not introduce advanced topics so early that beginners lose the core skill.

### 4.4 Questions that reveal understanding

Prefer questions such as:
- “Show me how you know this file was saved.”
- “What would happen if you selected the wrong disk?”
- “Why should we check this website's domain?”
- “Which part of the prompt did you change, and why?”
- “How did you verify this AI-generated formula?”
- “What can this website do, and what can it not do?”

Accept correct explanations in simple words. Correct terminology can be developed gradually.

## 5. Computer lab management

### 5.1 Room and workstation setup

Before students arrive:
- Check room access, lighting, ventilation, seating and clear walkways.
- Position screens so students can see the demonstration.
- Check that power cables are undamaged and routed safely.
- Ensure sockets and extension boards are not overloaded.
- Keep liquids away from equipment.
- Confirm computers boot, keyboards/mice work and displays are readable.
- Check internet access and approved software.
- Prepare a spare workstation or offline activity where possible.
- Ensure emergency exits and first-aid arrangements follow academy policy.

Do not daisy-chain extension boards or allow students to repair electrical equipment.

### 5.2 Device and account management

Maintain an inventory with academy asset label, make/model, condition, assigned workstation and fault notes. Use standard student accounts with only the permissions required. Keep administrator credentials with authorized staff; never write passwords on the board or share them with students.

Use separate instructor demonstration accounts and fictional data. Clear downloads and sign out of accounts at the end of a session according to academy policy. Do not leave an unlocked instructor account on a shared computer.

### 5.3 Start-of-class and end-of-class routines

**Start:** students sit at assigned workstations, inspect for visible damage, report faults, log in using approved accounts and open only the files needed for the lesson.

**End:** students save work to the approved location, close applications, sign out, safely eject removable storage when appropriate, shut down or leave devices in the academy's required state, return peripherals and report problems. The instructor checks that no personal account or sensitive file remains open.

### 5.4 Fault reporting

Students should report symptoms, not attempt repairs. Record device label, time, symptom, error message, recent change and steps already taken. Do not open a computer case, change firmware settings, install drivers or reset network equipment without instructor authorization.

## 6. Hardware handling and electrical safety

### 6.1 General rules

- Shut down the computer properly and disconnect power before opening a desktop case.
- Follow the manufacturer's instructions and academy risk assessment.
- Use an appropriate anti-static approach; avoid working on carpet and avoid touching connector contacts.
- Hold circuit boards by their edges.
- Keep screws and small parts in a tray.
- Never force RAM, cards, connectors or cables.
- Never open a power supply unit (PSU); dangerous electrical energy can remain inside.
- Do not open monitors, power adapters or other sealed electrical equipment.
- Stop if a component is hot, damaged, wet, swollen, sparking or smells burnt.
- Students should not work on live internal components.

### 6.2 Supervised component practice

Use a dedicated spare training desktop that can be safely opened. Demonstrate the correct way to identify the motherboard, CPU cooler, RAM, storage, PSU, GPU, fans and ports. If RAM removal/reinstallation is part of the activity, instructor checks power isolation and compatibility first, then supervises every student turn.

Never use a student's everyday computer or an academy production device for internal hardware practice.

### 6.3 Incident response

If there is smoke, burning smell, electrical shock, sparking or liquid spill:
1. Stop the activity and keep students away.
2. Do not touch exposed conductors or damaged equipment.
3. Isolate power only if it is safe and can be done without contact with danger.
4. Follow academy emergency procedures and contact qualified support.
5. Record and report the incident. Do not restart the device until it has been checked.

## 7. Windows, storage and installation safety

### 7.1 Ordinary Windows practice

Students may practise ordinary navigation, file management and approved settings using standard accounts. They should not change security settings, install unknown software, disable protection or change administrator settings.

### 7.2 Disk Management: view-only

Student activities in Disk Management are view-only. Do not allow students to:
- Format or delete a volume.
- Initialize or convert a disk.
- Shrink, extend or resize a partition.
- Change drive letters or paths.
- Accept an unexpected prompt to initialize a disk.

Explain that C: and D: may be volumes on one physical disk. A separate folder on the same disk is not a reliable backup.

### 7.3 Windows installation: controlled environment only

Installation practice is allowed only on a disposable virtual machine or a dedicated spare training computer under direct supervision. Never use a student's everyday computer, family computer or academy production device.

Before starting, the instructor verifies:
- Permission and ownership.
- A separate, verified backup and recovery plan.
- Correct target device by model and capacity.
- Official Windows installation media and approved version.
- Valid licensing/activation requirements.
- Power, network, drivers and time.
- VM snapshot or recovery media where applicable.
- Instructor control of disk selection and partition actions.

At the disk selection screen, the instructor confirms the target. If the screen differs from the lesson or any doubt exists, stop. Do not guess. Installation media does not itself provide a Windows licence. Do not use unofficial activation tools or modified images.

If the academy cannot provide a safe VM/spare PC, teach the process using an instructor demonstration, approved recording or annotated screenshots. Do not improvise on student devices.

## 8. Internet, email and network safety

- Use only academy-approved networks and devices.
- Do not access neighbouring networks or test network security.
- Do not display Wi-Fi passwords, router admin pages, public IP details or network credentials.
- Use fictional phishing examples; never ask students to open real suspicious links.
- Do not use real student inboxes for demonstrations.
- Do not save passwords on shared/public lab computers.
- Verify the exact website domain before entering credentials.
- HTTPS encrypts data in transit but does not prove that a site is honest.
- Unexpected urgency, suspicious links, attachments and requests for passwords or codes should be treated cautiously.
- Verify unusual requests using a known official channel and report concerns to the instructor.

For email practice, use paper templates or an instructor-controlled test account if student accounts are unavailable or inappropriate.

## 9. AI account, privacy and responsible-use management

### 9.1 Before using an AI service

Check current provider terms, minimum age, regional availability, account requirements, privacy settings and academy permission. Do not ask a student to bypass an age restriction or use another person's account. Where an individual account is not appropriate, use an instructor-led demonstration or an approved classroom alternative.

Feature names, free limits, model access, data controls and interfaces change. Verify them before teaching; prepare a non-AI alternative.

### 9.2 Information students must not submit

Do not enter:
- Passwords, verification codes, recovery keys or API keys.
- Identity documents, home addresses or private phone numbers.
- Private student records, marks, attendance or disciplinary details.
- Confidential academy documents or unpublished sensitive business information.
- Private photos, recordings or other people's personal information without permission.

Use fictional examples and synthetic data. Do not upload a real student spreadsheet to an AI tool to demonstrate formula generation.

### 9.3 Teaching AI responsibly

Explain that AI can produce incorrect, incomplete, biased or fabricated answers. Students should:
1. Check important facts against reliable sources.
2. Open and inspect cited sources rather than trusting a citation label.
3. Test spreadsheet formulas with known values.
4. Preview generated documents, slides, images and code.
5. Avoid copying an answer they cannot explain.
6. Disclose meaningful AI assistance where the assignment asks for it.
7. Ask a human instructor for decisions involving safety, privacy, money, health or important personal matters.

Teach students to request concise explanations and verifiable steps. Do not teach them to reveal hidden internal reasoning or confidential system instructions.

### 9.4 Prompt Coach classroom use

Prompt Coach helps turn a rough idea into a clearer prompt. Students should review the rewritten prompt, check that it preserves their intention, identify assumptions, remove invented details, choose the version they understand, test it and refine the result. It is a helper, not a guarantee of accuracy.

### 9.5 AI images, audio and video

Teach consent, copyright, attribution and impersonation risks. Do not generate deceptive content about a real person, clone a person's voice without permission or publish a student's image/voice without the academy's required consent. Label synthetic media where appropriate and follow the platform's current rules.

## 10. Software, licensing and access alternatives

Use licensed Microsoft Office where available. LibreOffice may be used for compatible Word/Excel/PowerPoint learning outcomes when Office is not available; explain that menus and file compatibility can differ.

Use official vendor websites for downloads. Do not install cracked software, unofficial activation tools, pirated fonts or unknown browser extensions. Check whether a tool requires an account, age eligibility, payment, a particular device or reliable internet.

Prepare alternatives:
- No internet: printed diagrams, offline practice files, local office software and instructor screenshots.
- No AI account: instructor demonstration, prepared sample outputs and manual verification activities.
- No Microsoft Office: LibreOffice.
- No suitable VM: instructor-led Windows installation demonstration only.
- No deployment access: local website plus repository-ready files and a deployment worksheet; never invent a live URL.

### 11.5 Fair marking

- Share task expectations before students begin.
- Award marks for demonstrated understanding and safe process, not speed alone.
- Accept equivalent correct explanations.
- Use the separate instructor answer key.
- Give feedback that names one strength and one next step.
- Provide a reasonable second attempt after reteaching.
- Apply the same rubric to all students.
- Keep individual marks private.

For safety-critical tasks, unsafe actions require the instructor to stop the activity and reteach. A student should not receive full practical credit for an unsafe process even if the final screen looks correct.

## 12. Student progress tracking and intervention

Maintain a confidential record for each student. Track:
- Attendance and punctuality.
- Lesson completion and workbook evidence.
- Practical skills: needs support / developing / independent.
- Quiz and assignment results.
- Safe equipment and account habits.
- Homework completion.
- Support or accessibility needs shared through approved channels.
- Feedback, intervention and reassessment date.

### Suggested skill scale

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Needs support | Cannot yet complete the task even with normal prompts |
| Developing | Completes some steps with a checklist or instructor help |
| Independent | Completes the task safely and explains the result |
| Can explain/transfer | Completes it independently and applies it to a new example |

### Intervention process
1. Identify the specific skill gap from observed evidence.
2. Discuss it privately and respectfully with the student.
3. Demonstrate the skill again using smaller steps.
4. Provide a short targeted practice task.
5. Recheck the skill within the next lesson or agreed review date.
6. Record the result and any further support needed.

Do not label a student as weak or incapable. Record the skill and support, not a judgement about the person.

## 13. Troubleshooting classroom learning

| Situation | Instructor response |
|---|---|
| Student cannot follow the projected steps | Pause, return to the last completed step and model one action at a time |
| Student saves work in the wrong folder | Demonstrate search and save-as; help them move a copy without deleting the original |
| Computer is slow | Observe Task Manager; check workload; avoid ending unknown processes |
| Device is missing in Device Manager | Record the device/status and escalate; do not install random drivers |
| Disk warning or unfamiliar prompt appears | Stop all clicks; do not initialize, format or delete; call instructor |
| Installation screen differs | Stop and compare with the prepared plan; never guess at disk selection |
| Wi-Fi connected but no website opens | Check approved connection, another device and a trusted site; report the error |
| AI gives a false answer | Verify against a reliable source; show how to revise the prompt and correct the result |
| AI tool unavailable | Use prepared output, offline worksheet or instructor demonstration |
| Office file will not open | Preserve the original; check file extension and app compatibility; use a copy for troubleshooting |
| Student shares a password/code | Stop, advise them not to share it, follow account recovery/reporting policy and inform authorized staff |
| Student opens a suspicious link | Stop interaction, do not enter credentials, notify instructor/IT and follow academy incident procedure |

For suspected malware, account compromise, data loss or privacy breach, stop using the affected device/account and follow the academy's incident-response process. Do not investigate beyond your authorization.

## 16. Course policies

### Attendance and participation
Follow the academy's published attendance policy. Students should notify the instructor about absence through the approved channel and complete missed essential practical work where possible. Participation means trying the task, asking questions, supporting respectful teamwork and keeping evidence of learning.

### Academic honesty and AI disclosure
Students must submit work they understand. Copying another student's work, submitting AI output without review where independent work is required, inventing sources or misrepresenting a task as completed is not acceptable. Students should disclose meaningful AI assistance and be able to explain their decisions.

### Privacy and publication
Use fictional data in examples. Obtain required permission before publishing academy names, logos, photographs, student work or identifiable information. Public repositories and websites can be copied or indexed; never publish confidential information or credentials.

### Equipment and software
Only authorized staff may install software, change administrator settings, open equipment for repair or perform destructive disk operations. Students must report faults and follow the lab routines.

### Respect and safeguarding
Treat all learners fairly. Do not tolerate bullying, harassment, discrimination, intimidation or sharing of private information. Follow academy safeguarding and escalation procedures. Do not promise confidentiality where a safety concern must be reported to authorized staff.

### Course improvement
After each week, review quiz patterns, practical evidence, student feedback, accessibility and timing. Make corrections to examples or instructions without changing the approved learning outcomes unless the academy formally approves a curriculum revision. A classroom pilot or completed lesson is not proof of external accreditation.
