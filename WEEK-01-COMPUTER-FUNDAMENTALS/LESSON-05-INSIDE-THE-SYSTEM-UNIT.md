# Lesson 5 — Inside the System Unit

**Course:** Computer Fundamentals and Artificial Intelligence Foundation Course  
**Week:** 1 — Computer Fundamentals and Hardware  
**Duration:** 120 minutes | **Level:** Beginner  
**Practical mode:** Instructor demonstration followed by supervised hands-on identification

## 1. Learning outcomes
Students will be able to:
1. Identify the main internal components of a desktop computer.
2. Describe the basic function of each component.
3. Identify common external ports and connectors.
4. Follow basic electrical and electrostatic discharge (ESD) safety rules.
5. Explain why components must be compatible.

## 2. Student lecture notes

### System unit
The system unit is the computer case that contains important internal components. It is not the same as the whole computer: the monitor, keyboard, and mouse are usually separate external devices.

### Main internal components
- **Motherboard:** The main circuit board. It connects the CPU, memory, storage, and other components.
- **CPU (Central Processing Unit):** Executes instructions and performs general-purpose processing. It is often called the processor.
- **RAM (Random Access Memory):** Fast temporary working memory used by active programs and data. Its contents are normally lost when power is turned off.
- **HDD (Hard Disk Drive):** Storage device that uses rotating magnetic disks. It usually offers large capacity at a lower cost per unit of storage.
- **SSD (Solid-State Drive):** Storage device using flash memory and no moving disk parts. It is generally faster than a traditional HDD.
- **PSU (Power Supply Unit):** Converts incoming electrical power into the forms needed by computer components.
- **GPU (Graphics Processing Unit):** Handles graphics and other parallel processing tasks. It may be built into the CPU or installed as a separate card.
- **Network interface:** Allows the computer to connect to a network through Ethernet or wireless technology.
- **Cooling system:** Fans and heatsinks help remove heat from components.
- **CMOS battery:** Helps maintain the real-time clock and certain firmware settings when the computer is unplugged.

### Ports and connectors
Common external connections include USB, HDMI, DisplayPort, Ethernet, audio, and power. Internal connections may include SATA data/power connectors and motherboard power connectors. Ports vary by computer model.

### Compatibility
A component must match the motherboard, case, power supply, and other system requirements. For example, RAM must be supported by the motherboard. Never force a component into a slot or connector.

## 3. Essential safety rules

1. Shut down the computer properly.
2. Switch off and disconnect the power cable before opening the case.
3. Follow the instructor's lab procedure and use a suitable work surface.
4. Reduce static electricity risk: handle components by their edges and use an ESD wrist strap or approved grounding method when available.
5. Never touch exposed contacts unnecessarily.
6. Never open a power supply unit (PSU). It can contain dangerous electrical components even when unplugged.
7. Never work inside a powered computer.
8. Do not force components, connectors, screws, or cables.
9. Keep liquids and food away from equipment.
10. Students must not open their personal computers without permission and supervision.

## 5. Supervised practical

**Equipment:** One instructor-approved spare desktop, component diagram, labels, suitable screwdriver, ESD protection where available.

### Part A — External inspection
Students identify the case, power button, USB ports, display output, Ethernet port, and audio connections.

### Part B — Internal inspection
The instructor first demonstrates safe shutdown, power disconnection, case opening, and safe handling. Students then work in small groups to identify:
- Motherboard
- CPU heatsink/fan
- RAM modules
- Storage drive
- PSU (identify only; do not open)
- GPU, if installed
- Network card, if separate
- Case fans
- CMOS battery
- SATA and power cables

### Part C — RAM demonstration
Only on a suitable training desktop and under direct instructor supervision:
1. Confirm power is disconnected.
2. Review the motherboard manual or labels.
3. Demonstrate the RAM retaining clips.
4. Remove and reinstall a compatible RAM module carefully.
5. Confirm it is seated correctly before closing the case.

If equipment, supervision, or safe conditions are not available, use a demonstration video or labelled component samples instead.

## 6. Check for understanding
1. What is the motherboard's main role?
2. What does the CPU do?
3. Why is RAM called temporary memory?
4. What is one difference between an HDD and an SSD?
5. What does the PSU do?
6. What is the purpose of a CPU heatsink and fan?
7. Why must you disconnect power before opening a desktop?
8. Why must students never open a PSU?
9. Name three external ports.
10. What should you do if a component does not fit easily?

## 7. Homework
1. Draw or print a system-unit diagram and label at least eight components.
2. Write one or two sentences explaining the function of each labelled component.
3. List five safety rules for working inside a computer.
4. Compare HDD and SSD in a small table.

## 8. Instructor notes
- Inspect the training computer before class. Remove damaged equipment from use.
- Use an intact, known-good spare computer, not a student's everyday device.
- Keep screws and small parts in a tray.
- Never open a PSU or allow students to handle exposed mains-voltage components.
- Do not require component removal if the machine is fragile, under warranty, or not suitable for training.
- Confirm the machine works before and after the activity.

## 9. Lesson completion checklist
- [ ] Student can identify at least eight internal components.
- [ ] Student can explain the function of CPU, RAM, motherboard, storage and PSU.
- [ ] Student can identify common external ports.
- [ ] Student follows power-disconnection and ESD precautions.
- [ ] Student understands that the PSU must never be opened.
