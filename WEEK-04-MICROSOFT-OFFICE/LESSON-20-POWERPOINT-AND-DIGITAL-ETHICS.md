# Lesson 20 — PowerPoint and Digital Ethics

**Week:** 4 | **Duration:** 120 minutes | **Practical focus:** 80%

## Learning outcomes
Students can create a short presentation, choose layouts and themes, insert text and visual elements, use transitions and animations sparingly, add speaker notes, present a slideshow and apply basic copyright, attribution and digital ethics principles.

## Key terms
Presentation, slide, layout, theme, transition, animation, speaker notes, copyright, public domain, Creative Commons, attribution, plagiarism.

## 1. What is presentation software?
Presentation software organizes information into slides for an audience. Microsoft PowerPoint is widely used. LibreOffice Impress and other tools are alternatives. A presentation should support a speaker; it should not be a page of tiny text.

## 2. Plan the story
Before opening the software, decide:
- Who is the audience?
- What should they learn or do?
- What are the three to five main points?
- What evidence or examples will help?

A simple six-slide structure:
1. Title and purpose.
2. Why the topic matters.
3. Key idea one.
4. Key idea two.
5. Practical example or checklist.
6. Summary and sources.

## 3. Slides, layouts and themes
Use a title slide and suitable content layouts. A theme provides coordinated colours and fonts. Keep headings and body text consistent. Leave enough empty space. Use a readable contrast between text and background.

## 4. Add visual elements
Insert relevant images, shapes, tables or charts. Use images that you created or have permission to use. Crop carefully and do not distort proportions. Charts should show accurate data and labels. Avoid decorative images that do not support the message.

## 5. Transitions and animations
A transition occurs between slides. An animation affects an object on a slide. Use these features only when they help the audience follow the explanation. Too many effects distract and can make a presentation look unprofessional.

## 6. Speaker notes and presenting
Add short speaker notes to remind yourself of examples or explanations. Practise in Slide Show mode. Check slide order, spelling, links, media and display compatibility. Do not read every slide word for word.

## 7. Copyright and responsible digital use
Copyright protects many creative works. Finding an image through a search engine does not mean it is free to reuse. Check the licence and follow its conditions. Public domain works may be reusable, but confirm the status and source.

Creative Commons licences have different conditions. Some require attribution; some restrict commercial use or adaptations; some require adaptations to use the same licence. Read the exact licence. Record creator, title, source and licence where required.

Plagiarism means presenting another person's work or ideas as your own without appropriate acknowledgement. Cite sources for facts, quotations, images and data. Do not invent sources. AI-generated content also needs human review; follow the academy's policy and disclose AI assistance when required.

## Guided practical — Six-slide technology safety presentation
Create six slides for beginner computer students. Include a title, three practical safety habits, one relevant permitted visual, a simple checklist, a summary and a source slide. Add speaker notes to at least two slides. Use a consistent theme, readable text and no distracting animations. Present for two minutes.

## 120-minute teaching plan
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–20 min | Explain presentation purpose, audience and slide planning |
| 20–40 min | Demonstrate themes, layouts and slide order |
| 40–65 min | Students build title and content slides |
| 65–85 min | Add images, shapes, tables or chart |
| 85–95 min | Speaker notes and restrained effects |
| 95–108 min | Copyright, source slide and review |
| 108–120 min | Short presentations and feedback |

## Understanding check
1. What is the difference between a transition and an animation?
2. Why should a slide not contain a full essay?
3. Does an image appearing in search results mean it is free to use?
4. What information may be needed in an attribution?
5. Why should AI-generated facts and sources be checked?

## Homework
Prepare a five-slide presentation titled **How to Use the Internet Safely**. Include a source slide, at least one properly attributed visual, and speaker notes on two slides.

## Instructor notes
Teach attribution as a practical habit, not a legal guarantee. Use instructor-approved images and sample licences. Students should not upload private information or images of classmates without permission. Assess clarity and accuracy, not visual effects.

- [ ] Apply a consistent theme and layouts.
- [ ] Insert suitable visual elements.
- [ ] Use transitions/animations sparingly.
- [ ] Add speaker notes and present.
