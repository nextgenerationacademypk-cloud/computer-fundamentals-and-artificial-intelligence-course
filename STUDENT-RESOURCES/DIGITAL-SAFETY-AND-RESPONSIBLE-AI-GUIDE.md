# Digital Safety and Responsible AI — Student Guide

This guide explains how to protect yourself, your files, your accounts and other people while using computers and AI.

## 1. Protect your accounts

- Use a strong, unique password for each important account.
- Do not share your password with classmates or anyone who asks unexpectedly.
- Never share a one-time verification code or recovery code.
- Use multi-factor authentication when available and approved.
- Sign out of shared computers.
- Do not save passwords in a public or shared browser.
- Ask the instructor or an authorized adult for help if you think an account is compromised.

An instructor should never ask you to announce your password to the class.

## 2. Recognize phishing

Phishing is a trick used to make someone reveal information, open a harmful file or visit a fake website.

Warning signs can include:
- A message creates fear or urgency: “Your account closes in 10 minutes!”
- The sender address looks strange or does not match the organization.
- A link uses a misspelled or look-alike website name.
- An unexpected attachment asks you to enable macros or install something.
- The message asks for a password, verification code or payment.
- A prize or offer sounds too good to be true.

**Safe response:** Do not click, open or reply. Check through a separate official channel and tell your instructor. Do not forward suspicious messages to classmates.

## 3. Check websites and links

- Read the domain carefully before signing in.
- A logo or professional design does not prove that a site is genuine.
- HTTPS encrypts information while it travels between your browser and the website. It does not prove that the website is honest.
- Use trusted sources and compare important information.
- Avoid unexpected downloads and unknown browser extensions.

## 4. Keep your files safe

- Save your work in the correct course folder.
- Use clear file names and save regularly.
- Keep a backup copy in an approved separate location.
- Do not delete files when you are unsure what they do.
- Do not format or change disk partitions during student practice.
- Ask before connecting unknown USB drives.

A folder on the same physical disk is not a safe backup if that disk fails.

## 5. Protect privacy

Do not share publicly:
- Passwords, verification codes or recovery keys.
- Home address or private phone number.
- Identity documents.
- Private student marks, attendance or contact details.
- Private photographs or recordings of another person.
- Confidential academy documents.

Think before you upload or publish. Public posts and repositories can be copied.

## 6. Use AI responsibly

AI tools can make mistakes, show outdated information or invent sources. They do not replace your judgement.

Before using an AI answer:
1. Read it carefully.
2. Check facts using reliable sources.
3. Open and inspect cited references.
4. Test formulas with known values.
5. Run and inspect code before publishing it.
6. Remove private information from your prompt.
7. Ask your instructor if the answer affects safety or an important decision.

### Never enter these into an AI tool
- Passwords or one-time codes.
- Personal student records.
- Private contact information.
- Identity documents.
- Confidential academy files.
- Another person's private photo, voice or message without permission.

Use fictional examples for practice.

## 7. AI images, voices and videos

- Do not make a fake image or video to trick people.
- Do not imitate a real person's voice without permission.
- Do not publish another person's image or voice without required permission.
- Respect copyright and creator licences.
- Give credit where required.
- Make it clear when media is AI-generated if the task or platform requires it.

## 8. Safe publishing checklist

Before sharing a document, image, video, website or GitHub repository, check:

- [ ] No password, secret key or verification code is included.
- [ ] No private student information is included.
- [ ] Images and text are allowed to be used.
- [ ] Names and photos are approved for publication.
- [ ] Links work and lead to the intended site.
- [ ] AI-generated facts and code have been checked.
- [ ] The work does not mislead viewers.
- [ ] The instructor has reviewed it if academy branding or student identity is involved.

## 9. If something goes wrong

Stop. Do not hide the problem or keep clicking. Tell your instructor. If a password or code was shared, follow the account recovery instructions from the official service and the academy. If you see harmful content, bullying, threats or a privacy problem, report it to the instructor or the academy's approved safeguarding contact.

**Remember:** Stop, check, ask and report. A safe decision is more important than finishing quickly.
